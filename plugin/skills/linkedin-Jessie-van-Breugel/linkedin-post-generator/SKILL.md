# LinkedIn Post Generator

## What This Skill Does

Writes complete, ready-to-post LinkedIn content for people who sell expertise — coaches, consultants, trainers, and B2B service providers. Every post is engineered to attract potential clients, not just likes.

The distinction matters: a "viral" post that gets 50K impressions from other content creators is worthless if none of them would ever buy from you. A post that gets 2,000 impressions but puts 3 ideal clients in your DMs is a winner.

---

## The Post Architecture

Every effective LinkedIn post follows this structure, regardless of type:

### 1. HOOK (Lines 1-3)
Stops the scroll. Creates enough tension or recognition to earn the "see more" click. Under 210 characters.

### 2. BODY (Lines 4-20)
Delivers the value. Teaches, tells, proves, or reframes something. This is where trust is built.

### 3. PAYOFF (Lines 21-25)
Lands the insight. The "so what" — what should the reader think, feel, or believe differently after reading this?

### 4. CTA (Final lines)
Tells the reader what to do next. One action. Clear and specific.

## The 6 Post Types

When someone asks for a post, identify which type fits their topic. If they don't specify, recommend the best fit.

### TYPE 1: THE TEACHING POST
**Purpose:** Demonstrate expertise by solving a small problem.
**Best for:** Frameworks, how-tos, step-by-step breakdowns.

**Structure:**
- Hook: Pain statement or "most people get this wrong"
- Body: 3-5 clear steps or a simple framework
- Payoff: What changes when they implement this
- CTA: "Save this for later" or "DM me [WORD] for the full guide"

**Example skeleton:**
```
[Hook: Common mistake or pain point]

Here's what actually works:

Step 1: [Action + why]
Step 2: [Action + why]
Step 3: [Action + why]

[Payoff: What this looks like when it's working]

[CTA: One specific next step]
```

**Guidelines:**
- Teach ONE thing well. Not five things poorly.
- Use their language, not your framework jargon
- Each step should be actionable TODAY — not "develop a strategy"
- Include a specific example or number wherever possible

### TYPE 2: THE STORY POST
**Purpose:** Build connection and trust through a specific moment.
**Best for:** Client wins, personal lessons, behind-the-scenes moments.

**Structure:**
- Hook: Drop into a moment (time, place, what happened)
- Body: The situation → the tension → the resolution
- Payoff: The lesson or insight
- CTA: Question or invitation to share their experience

**Example skeleton:**
```
[Hook: Specific moment — "Last Tuesday, a client sent me a voice note at 11pm."]

[Context: Who they are, what they were struggling with — 2-3 sentences]

[The turn: What changed, what they did, what happened]

[The result: Specific outcome with numbers if possible]

[The lesson: What this means for the reader]

[CTA: "Has this happened to you?" or "DM me [WORD] if you want to know how"]
```

**Guidelines:**
- Start in the middle of the action, not the background
- One story per post. Don't combine two lessons.
- If it's a client story, anonymize unless you have permission
- The lesson should be transferable — the reader should see themselves in it

### TYPE 3: THE OPINION POST
**Purpose:** Attract people who share your values and repel those who don't.
**Best for:** Contrarian takes, industry commentary, belief-shifting content.

**Structure:**
- Hook: The opinion, stated directly
- Body: Why you believe this + evidence or experience
- Payoff: What happens when you adopt this belief
- CTA: Invite agreement/disagreement or ask for their take

**Example skeleton:**
```
[Hook: Strong opinion — no hedging]

[Why: Personal experience or client evidence — 3-5 sentences]

[The alternative: What happens when people do the opposite]

[Restatement: The opinion again, with nuance]

[CTA: "Agree or disagree? Tell me in the comments."]
```

**Guidelines:**
- State the opinion in the first line. Don't build up to it.
- Back it with experience, not theory
- Don't be contrarian for the sake of it — only share opinions you've earned
- It's fine if some people disagree. That's the point.

### TYPE 4: THE CASE STUDY POST
**Purpose:** Show proof that your approach works.
**Best for:** Client results, before/after transformations, ROI stories.

**Structure:**
- Hook: The result (number, transformation, timeframe)
- Body: Where they started → what you did → where they ended
- Payoff: Why this matters for the reader
- CTA: "Want similar results? DM me [WORD]"

**Example skeleton:**
```
[Hook: "12 inbound leads in 30 days. Here's how a leadership coach did it with 800 followers."]

Before:
[Where the client was — specific pain, numbers, situation]

What we changed:
[2-3 specific actions taken — not vague "we worked on their strategy"]

After:
[Specific results — numbers, timeline, client quote if available]

[Payoff: What this means for someone in a similar situation]

[CTA: Direct invitation]
```

**Guidelines:**
- Lead with the result, not the backstory
- Be specific: "12 leads in 30 days" not "significant improvement"
- Show the WHAT, not the entire HOW (save the full method for your program)
- Always tie it back to the reader: "If you're a [similar person], here's what this means for you"

### TYPE 5: THE HANDRAISER POST
**Purpose:** Generate DMs and identify potential clients.
**Best for:** Offering a free resource, audit, or direct help.

**Structure:**
- Hook: Problem statement or valuable offer
- Body: What you're offering and why (brief)
- Payoff: What they'll get specifically
- CTA: "Comment [WORD] and I'll send it" or "DM me [WORD]"

**Example skeleton:**
```
[Hook: "I put together a [specific resource] that solves [specific problem]."]

[What it includes — 3 bullet points, specific]

[Who it's for — name the ICP]

[CTA: "Comment CHECKLIST and I'll DM it to you."]
```

**Guidelines:**
- The resource should solve a SMALL problem, not the whole thing
- Name the ICP explicitly so the wrong people self-select out
- "Comment [WORD]" outperforms "DM me" for reach (comments boost the algorithm)
- Deliver within 24 hours. Speed builds trust.

### TYPE 6: THE BELIEF SHIFT POST
**Purpose:** Move the reader from an old way of thinking to a new one.
**Best for:** Reframing problems, challenging assumptions, seeding your methodology.

**Structure:**
- Hook: The old belief stated plainly
- Body: Why it's wrong + what the new belief is + evidence
- Payoff: What changes when they adopt the new belief
- CTA: Question or invitation

**Example skeleton:**
```
[Hook: "Most consultants think they need more content. They don't."]

[The old way: What most people believe and do — 2-3 sentences]

[The problem: Why this doesn't work — specific evidence]

[The new way: What actually works — your reframe]

[Payoff: What shifts when they think this way]

[CTA: Question or resource offer]
```

## Formatting Rules for LinkedIn

- **Short paragraphs.** 1-3 sentences per block. Never a wall of text.
- **Line breaks between every paragraph.** LinkedIn compresses text — space it out.
- **No hashtags in the body.** If used at all, put 3-5 at the very end. They don't help discovery much and they clutter the post.
- **No emojis as bullet points.** Use them sparingly if at all. They signal "content creator" not "expert."
- **Bold text doesn't work on LinkedIn.** Don't format with asterisks expecting bold.
- **Ideal length:** 800-1,300 characters for text posts. Longer for stories and case studies, shorter for opinions.
- **One idea per post.** If you're covering two topics, that's two posts.

---

## Voice Guidelines

The post should sound like the user, not like AI. To achieve this:

- Ask about their natural tone (casual, professional, somewhere in between)
- Mirror their vocabulary and sentence rhythm
- Avoid AI tells: "In today's fast-paced world," "Let me share," "Here's the thing," "At the end of the day"
- Avoid generic motivational language: "Don't give up," "Believe in yourself," "Success is a journey"
- Read the draft out loud — if it sounds like a blog post or a textbook, rewrite it
- Short sentences mixed with longer ones. Never uniform length.

## Output Format

When delivering a post, present it as:

```
**Post Type:** [Which of the 6 types]
**Target Reader:** [Who should stop scrolling for this]

---

[Full post text, formatted for LinkedIn with proper line breaks]

---

**Why this works:** [2-3 sentences explaining the strategic thinking]
**Suggested posting time:** [Optional — based on their audience if known]
```

If the user provides a rough idea or draft, generate 2 versions: one close to their original direction and one that takes a different angle on the same topic. Let them pick.

## Common Requests and How to Handle Them

- **"I don't know what to post"** → Ask: What's the last thing a client thanked you for? What question do you get asked most? What mistake do you see your clients making? Any of these is a post.
- **"Make it go viral"** → Reframe: viral is not the goal. Reaching the RIGHT 500 people is better than reaching 50,000 wrong ones. Write for your future clients, not for impressions.
- **"Can you write a week's worth?"** → Generate 3-5 posts covering different types. Recommend a mix: 2 teaching, 1 story, 1 opinion, 1 handraiser.
- **"My posts get likes but no leads"** → The issue is usually the CTA or the targeting. Review who the post attracts (other creators vs. potential clients) and whether the CTA creates a next step.

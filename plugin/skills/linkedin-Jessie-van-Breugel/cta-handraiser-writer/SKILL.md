# CTA & Handraiser Writer

## What This Skill Does

Writes the endings of LinkedIn posts — the part that turns a reader into a potential client conversation. Most experts write great content and then either end with no CTA (leaving money on the table) or slap on a "book a call" link (scaring everyone away).

This skill finds the middle ground: CTAs that feel like a natural extension of the value, not a pitch.

---

## The Handraiser Concept

A handraiser is someone who takes a small action that signals they might be a potential client — without requiring them to commit to anything big. It's a low-friction way for the right people to identify themselves.

**Traditional funnel:** Post → "Book a call" → Most people scroll past
**Handraiser funnel:** Post → "Comment GUIDE and I'll send it" → They comment → You DM them → Conversation starts naturally

The handraiser is the bridge between content and conversation. It works because:
- It's low commitment (a comment or DM, not a call)
- It's value-first (they get something useful)
- It identifies intent (they're interested in the topic = potential fit)
- It gives you a reason to DM them (you're delivering what they asked for, not cold pitching)

## The 7 CTA Types

### TYPE 1: THE RESOURCE HANDRAISER
**When to use:** After a teaching post, framework post, or any post that solves a small problem.
**How it works:** Offer a deeper resource related to the post. The comment or DM is the gateway.

**Templates:**
```
"I put this into a [checklist/template/guide] you can use. Comment [WORD] and I'll send it over."

"Want the full [framework/system/breakdown]? DM me [WORD] and I'll share it."

"I made a [1-pager/cheat sheet/swipe file] with all [X] of these. Comment [WORD] below."
```

**Rules:**
- The keyword should be relevant to the content (AUDIT for a profile post, HOOKS for a hook post)
- "Comment" outperforms "DM me" because comments boost the post's reach
- The resource must be real and delivered within 24 hours
- Make the resource specific: "my 5-step profile checklist" not "a free resource"

### TYPE 2: THE QUESTION CTA
**When to use:** After an opinion post, story post, or belief-shift post.
**How it works:** Ask a question that invites the reader to engage. The best questions are ones where the answer reveals whether they're a potential client.

**Templates:**
```
"What's the #1 thing holding you back from [the outcome your service delivers]? Drop it in the comments."

"Honest question: which of these [mistakes/stages/scenarios] are you in right now?"

"What would change in your business if you solved [the specific problem]? Genuinely curious."
```

**Rules:**
- The question should be answerable in 1-2 sentences (not an essay)
- It should reveal their situation — answers like "I'm stuck on positioning" tell you they're a potential fit
- Respond to every answer with a follow-up question (this starts a conversation)
- Never ask "Who agrees?" or "Anyone else?" — these are engagement bait, not lead generation

### TYPE 3: THE DIAGNOSIS CTA
**When to use:** After a post about common mistakes, stages of growth, or "which type are you" content.
**How it works:** Invite readers to self-identify where they are. This is powerful because it combines engagement with self-qualification.

**Templates:**
```
"Which of these 3 stages are you in right now? Drop your number below and I'll share what to focus on first."

"Be honest: are you more of a [Type A] or [Type B]? Your answer determines your next move."

"Rate yourself 1-10 on [the skill/area from the post]. If you're below a 6, I've got something that can help — DM me."
```

**Rules:**
- Keep the options to 2-3 (not 7)
- Each option should map to a real segment of your audience
- Have a follow-up ready for each response type
- The diagnosis should feel helpful, not judgmental

### TYPE 4: THE SOFT OFFER
**When to use:** After a case study, results post, or proof-heavy content.
**How it works:** Mention what you do and how to take the next step — but make it about THEM, not your service.

**Templates:**
```
"If you're in a similar spot to [client in the post] — dealing with [specific problem] — I might be able to help. DM me and let's see."

"This is exactly what we work on inside [your program/service]. If you want to know whether it'd make sense for your situation, send me a message."

"I've got [X] spots open this [month/quarter] for [what you do]. If the timing's right, DM me and we'll talk."
```

**Rules:**
- Only use after you've demonstrated real value or proof
- Keep it to 1-2 lines — don't write a sales page in your CTA
- "Let's see if it makes sense" is better than "book a call now"
- Never use urgency you can't back up — "only 2 spots left" only if that's actually true

### TYPE 5: THE SAVE & SHARE CTA
**When to use:** After a highly tactical or reference-worthy post.
**How it works:** Encourage saves and shares, which boost reach and put your content in front of new audiences.

**Templates:**
```
"Save this for the next time you sit down to write a [post/headline/DM]. It'll save you 30 minutes."

"If you know a [their ICP peer] who's dealing with this — send it their way."

"Bookmark this. You'll need it when [specific situation they'll face]."
```

**Rules:**
- Only ask to save/share when the content genuinely warrants it
- Be specific about WHEN they'll need it again — this increases save rates
- "Save this" works for tactical content; "share this" works for relatable content
- Don't combine save/share CTAs with handraiser CTAs — pick one

### TYPE 6: THE FOLLOW CTA
**When to use:** On your best-performing posts or when you're actively growing your audience.
**How it works:** Simple — tell them to follow for more of this type of content.

**Templates:**
```
"I break down [your topic] like this every [day/week]. Follow along if it's useful."

"More frameworks like this coming [this week/month]. Follow to catch them."
```

**Rules:**
- Use sparingly — max 1 in every 5-7 posts
- Be specific about what they'll get by following
- Pair with a resource handraiser if possible: "Follow for more, and comment GUIDE to grab the full checklist"

### TYPE 7: THE NO-CTA CTA
**When to use:** On opinion posts, vulnerable stories, or controversial takes.
**How it works:** Don't ask for anything. Just end. The post speaks for itself. The people who resonate will check your profile on their own.

**When it works:**
- When the post is emotionally powerful and a CTA would cheapen it
- When you've posted 3-4 handraiser posts in a row and need a break
- When the content is genuinely just a gift with no strings

**Rule:** Even with no explicit CTA, make sure your profile is optimized. The "CTA" is your profile doing its job when they click through.

## The CTA Calendar: Rotation Matters

Don't use the same CTA type every post. Rotate them:

| Day | Post Type | CTA Type |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Teaching | Resource Handraiser |
| Tue | Story | Question CTA |
| Wed | Opinion | No-CTA or Question |
| Thu | Framework/Tactical | Save CTA + Resource Handraiser |
| Fri | Case Study / Proof | Soft Offer |

**Guideline:** For every 5 posts:
- 2 resource handraisers
- 1 question CTA
- 1 save/share or follow CTA
- 1 soft offer or no-CTA

Never do 3 soft offers in a row. Your audience will tune out.

## Handraiser Keywords That Work

The keyword people comment should be:
- **Short** — one word, all caps
- **Relevant** — AUDIT for a profile post, HOOKS for a hooks post
- **Easy to remember** — not "LEADGENFRAMEWORK2024"
- **Unique per resource** — track which keywords drive the most DMs

**Good keywords:** GUIDE, CHECKLIST, AUDIT, TEMPLATE, SYSTEM, FRAMEWORK, PLAYBOOK, SWIPE, LIST, PLAN

**Bad keywords:** YES, ME, INTERESTED (too generic — you can't track what they wanted)

## Output Format

When writing CTAs, present them as:

```
**Post context:** [What the post is about]
**CTA type:** [Which of the 7]
**Goal:** [What action you want from the reader]

---

[CTA text — ready to paste at the end of the post]

---

**Follow-up action:** [What to do when they respond]
**Expected conversion:** [Rough guide — e.g., "Expect 2-5% of viewers to comment the keyword"]
```

Always provide 2-3 CTA options for the same post so the user can pick the one that matches their comfort level and audience relationship.

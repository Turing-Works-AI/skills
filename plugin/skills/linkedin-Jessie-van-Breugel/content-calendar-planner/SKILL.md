# Content Calendar Planner

## What This Skill Does

Creates a structured weekly and monthly LinkedIn content plan that maps directly to the user's offer and ideal client. Not a generic "post tips on Tuesday" plan — a strategic content system that moves readers from "interesting" to "I need to talk to this person."

Built for coaches, consultants, and service providers who know they should post but either don't know what to post, can't stay consistent, or post randomly and wonder why nothing converts.

---

## The Content Architecture

### Step 1: Define the 3 Content Pillars

Every effective LinkedIn content strategy runs on 3 pillars. Not 5. Not 7. Three. Each pillar maps to a stage in the buyer's journey:

**Pillar 1: PAIN (Awareness)**
Content that describes the problem your ICP has — in their language. Makes them feel seen and understood. This is top-of-funnel content that attracts the right people.

**Pillar 2: EDUCATION (Consideration)**
Content that teaches how to solve the problem — or a small piece of it. Demonstrates your expertise and approach. This is middle-of-funnel content that builds trust.

**Pillar 3: PROOF (Decision)**
Content that shows you've actually solved this problem for others. Case studies, client wins, results, testimonials. This is bottom-of-funnel content that creates urgency and confidence.

### How to Identify Pillars

Ask the user:
1. What's the #1 problem your clients have when they come to you?
2. What's the core method or approach you use to solve it?
3. What results do your clients typically get?

**Example for a leadership coach:**
- Pain: "Senior leaders who are great at execution but burning out because they can't delegate"
- Education: "The shift from 'doing leader' to 'enabling leader' — frameworks and tactics"
- Proof: "Client stories — leaders who reclaimed 10+ hours/week and grew their teams"

**Example for a B2B consultant:**
- Pain: "Consultants hitting the referral ceiling — network is tapped, growth has stalled"
- Education: "How to build an inbound system on LinkedIn — profile, content, DMs"
- Proof: "Client who went from 0 inbound to 12 leads/month in 60 days"

### Step 2: Set the Weekly Cadence

**Recommended posting frequency:** 3-5 posts per week. For most service providers, 4 is the sweet spot — enough to stay visible, not so much that quality drops.

**The Weekly Template:**

| Day | Pillar | Post Type | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | PAIN | Story or Opinion | Attract — make the right people feel seen |
| Tuesday | EDUCATION | Teaching or Framework | Trust — demonstrate expertise |
| Wednesday | PROOF | Case Study or Results | Convert — show it works |
| Thursday | EDUCATION | Carousel or How-To | Trust — go deeper, get saves |
| Friday | PAIN or EDUCATION | Handraiser | Capture — generate DMs |

**Alternate for 3x/week:**

| Day | Pillar | Post Type | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tuesday | PAIN | Story or Opinion | Attract |
| Thursday | EDUCATION | Teaching or Framework | Trust |
| Saturday | PROOF + Handraiser | Case Study with CTA | Convert + Capture |

### The Ratio Rule
Over any 2-week period:
- 40% Education (builds trust, gets saves)
- 30% Pain (attracts the right people)
- 20% Proof (converts interest into intent)
- 10% Direct offer or handraiser (captures leads)

If the content is all education, they'll learn from you but never buy. If it's all proof, they'll think you're bragging. If it's all pain, they'll feel seen but not helped. The mix matters.

### Step 3: Generate Topic Ideas

For each pillar, generate 4-6 specific topic ideas based on the user's inputs.

**Pain topics come from:**
- Questions clients ask before they buy
- Mistakes you see your ICP making
- Frustrations your ICP shares in discovery calls
- The moment they realize they need help

**Education topics come from:**
- Steps in your methodology (teach one step per post)
- Common misconceptions in your space
- Frameworks you use with clients
- "If I could only tell them one thing" insights

**Proof topics come from:**
- Client transformations (before → after)
- Specific results with numbers
- Client quotes or testimonials
- Behind-the-scenes of your process in action

## Monthly Planning: The 4-Week Cycle

For longer-term planning, use this monthly rhythm:

**Week 1: Problem Awareness**
Heavy on pain content. Surface the problem your ICP has. Make them realize the cost of inaction.

**Week 2: Solution Education**
Heavy on teaching. Share your framework, method, or approach. Give them enough to understand your thinking.

**Week 3: Proof & Social Evidence**
Heavy on case studies and results. Show that the solution works — for people like them.

**Week 4: Offer & Conversion**
Include your handraiser posts and soft offers. This is when you invite action — after 3 weeks of value.

This cycle can repeat every month. The topics change, but the rhythm stays the same.

## Content Batching Strategy

For users who want to batch-create content:

**The 2-Hour Content Block (creates 1 week of content):**
1. (10 min) Review your 3 pillars and the week's assigned types
2. (20 min) Write post 1 — start with the hook, fill in the body, add CTA
3. (20 min) Write post 2
4. (20 min) Write post 3
5. (20 min) Write post 4
6. (15 min) Write post 5 (handraiser — usually shortest)
7. (15 min) Review all 5 — read out loud, cut filler, sharpen hooks

**Tips:**
- Batch on the same day each week (Sunday or Friday works for most)
- Schedule posts using LinkedIn's native scheduler
- Don't edit during writing — write first, edit after
- Keep a running "topic bank" — add ideas whenever they come to you (client calls, comments, questions)

---

## The Topic Bank

Teach the user to maintain a running list of content ideas. Whenever they experience one of these, add it to the bank:

- A client asks a question → Teaching post
- A client gets a result → Case study post
- They see a bad take on LinkedIn → Opinion post
- They have a personal realization → Story post
- They overhear their ICP's frustration → Pain post
- They solve a problem for a client → Framework post

A good topic bank means never staring at a blank screen wondering "what should I post?"

## Input Needed From the User

To build a content calendar, gather:

1. **Who they serve** (ICP — role, industry, company size, situation)
2. **What they sell** (offer, price range, format)
3. **What result they deliver** (specific, measurable)
4. **How many times/week they want to post** (3, 4, or 5)
5. **How many weeks to plan** (1, 2, or 4)
6. **Any existing content or topics they've posted about** (optional — to avoid repetition)
7. **Any upcoming events, launches, or promotions to plan around** (optional)

## Common Situations

- **"I don't know who my ICP is yet"** → Help them narrow it down before planning content. Content without a clear ICP is content for everyone, which means content for no one. Ask: Who was your favorite client? Who pays the most? Who gets the best results? Start there.
- **"I tried posting consistently but nothing happened"** → Usually means the content wasn't strategic — just consistent. Check if they were mixing pillars, using CTAs, and writing for their ICP (not other creators).
- **"I can only post 2x/week"** → Fine. Do 1 education + 1 proof/handraiser per week. Quality over quantity. Two great posts beat five mediocre ones.
- **"Should I post on weekends?"** → Test it. Saturday morning posts often perform well because there's less competition. Sunday is usually dead. LinkedIn is a weekday platform for most B2B audiences.

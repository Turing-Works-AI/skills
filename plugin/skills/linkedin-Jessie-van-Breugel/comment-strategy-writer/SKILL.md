# Comment Strategy Writer

## What This Skill Does

Writes LinkedIn comments that make people think "Who is this person? I need to check out their profile." This is the most underrated lead generation tactic on LinkedIn — and for coaches, consultants, and service providers, it's often faster than creating content from scratch.

Here's the math: a thoughtful comment on a post with 10,000 views can put your name and face in front of thousands of your ideal clients — without writing a single post. And unlike your own posts where you're fighting the algorithm, comments on popular posts get free distribution.

The goal is NOT to get likes on your comment. The goal is to get profile clicks from the right people.

---

## Why Comments Work for Lead Generation

1. **Borrowed audience.** You appear in front of someone else's audience — people who already trust that creator. That trust partially transfers to you.
2. **Low effort, high visibility.** One great comment takes 3 minutes. A great post takes 30-60 minutes. The ROI per minute is often better.
3. **Profile visits convert.** When someone reads a smart comment and clicks your name, they land on your profile with a positive first impression. If your profile is optimized, that visit can turn into a follow, a DM, or a client.
4. **Relationship building.** Consistent, valuable comments on the same creators' posts build real relationships. Creators notice their regulars. This leads to collaborations, shoutouts, and referrals.

## The 5 Comment Types That Drive Profile Clicks

### TYPE 1: THE VALUE ADD
Adds a new insight, example, or angle that the original post didn't cover. This is the highest-performing comment type.

**Template:**
```
[Agree with the core point — 1 sentence]

[Add something new: a personal example, a data point, a different angle, or a practical tip that extends the original post]

[Optional: pose a follow-up question]
```

**Example (on a post about LinkedIn hooks):**
```
This is spot on. The hook is the whole game.

One thing I'd add: hooks that name a specific ICP ("If you're a B2B consultant with 500-2000 followers...") consistently outperform generic hooks in my testing. Not in total impressions — but in DMs. The people who DO read past the hook are exactly who you want.

What's your take on specificity vs. broad appeal in hooks?
```

**Why it works:** You're not just agreeing — you're extending the conversation with something only YOU would know. This signals expertise and makes people curious about what else you know.

### TYPE 2: THE PROOF POINT
Shares a real result, data point, or experience that validates or challenges the post's claim.

**Template:**
```
[Reference the specific claim]

[Share your proof — a result, a number, a client example, or personal experience]

[What it means or what you learned]
```

**Example (on a post about niching down):**
```
"Speaking to everyone means speaking to no one" — lived this.

Had a client who was positioning as a "business growth consultant." Zero inbound. We narrowed to "helping accounting firms scale past $1M without adding partners." First qualified lead within 3 weeks. Same expertise, completely different results.

The niche isn't a limitation — it's a magnet.
```

**Why it works:** Concrete proof in a comment stands out because most comments are opinion-based. A specific example with numbers is impossible to scroll past.

### TYPE 3: THE RESPECTFUL CHALLENGE
Disagrees with or nuances a point from the post — respectfully and with reasoning. This is high-risk, high-reward.

**Template:**
```
[Acknowledge what's valid about the point]

[Present your different perspective with evidence or reasoning]

[End constructively — not dismissively]
```

**Example (on a post claiming "DMs are dead"):**
```
I hear this take a lot, and I think there's a distinction worth making.

Cold DMs are dead — no argument there. Nobody wants a pitch slap from a stranger. But warm DMs — responding to someone who commented on your post, engaged with your content, or viewed your profile twice — that's a completely different conversation. 

Most of the high-ticket clients I've seen close recently came through warm DMs, not calls. I think the problem isn't DMs — it's the approach.
```

**Why it works:** Thoughtful disagreement drives more profile clicks than agreement. People are curious about who would push back on a popular creator. The key is being smart about it, not combative.

**Rules for challenges:**
- Always acknowledge what's valid first
- Provide evidence, not just opinion
- Never attack the creator personally
- End with a bridge, not a mic drop

### TYPE 4: THE STORY DROP
Shares a brief, relevant personal story that illustrates the post's point.

**Template:**
```
[Connect to the post — 1 sentence]

[Tell a quick story — specific, vivid, 3-5 sentences max]

[The lesson or takeaway]
```

**Example (on a post about imposter syndrome):**
```
This hit home.

Two years ago I nearly deleted my first LinkedIn post before hitting publish. "Who am I to talk about this? Everyone already knows this stuff." I posted it anyway. Three people DMed me. One became a client. She told me later: "Your post said exactly what I'd been thinking but couldn't articulate."

The people who need you most are the ones who feel seen by the obvious stuff.
```

**Why it works:** Stories are magnetic in comments because nobody else does them. A comment section full of "great post!" and "so true!" — then YOUR comment tells a vivid mini-story? That's a profile click.

### TYPE 5: THE PRACTICAL APPLICATION
Takes the post's advice and shows exactly how to implement it. Turns theory into action.

**Template:**
```
[Identify the key takeaway from the post]

[Show the practical application — step by step or as a specific example]

[Optional: offer to share more detail]
```

**Example (on a post about content pillars):**
```
Great framework. Here's how I'd apply this concretely:

If you're a leadership coach, your 3 pillars might be:
→ Pillar 1: Why high performers burn out (pain)
→ Pillar 2: The leadership shift from "doing" to "enabling" (education)
→ Pillar 3: Client transformations (proof)

Then you rotate: pain, education, proof, pain, education, proof. Every post maps to one. Keeps you focused and your audience clear on what you're about.
```

**Why it works:** You've just done the thinking for the reader. They don't have to figure out how to apply the advice — you showed them. This positions you as someone who makes things actionable, which is exactly what clients want.

## Commenting Strategy: Who, When, How Often

### WHO to comment on:
- **Creators whose audience overlaps with your ICP.** If you help leadership coaches, comment on posts by leadership influencers, HR thought leaders, and executive coaches with large followings.
- **Creators slightly bigger than you.** 5x-20x your audience is the sweet spot. They're big enough to give you exposure, small enough to notice you.
- **Your actual potential clients.** If an ideal client posts something, comment. This is the warmest engagement possible.
- **NOT other people in your exact same niche.** Their audience is mostly other service providers, not potential clients.

### WHEN to comment:
- **Within the first 60 minutes of a post going live.** Early comments get the most visibility. LinkedIn's algorithm pushes posts with early engagement, and your comment sits at the top.
- **Consistency > volume.** 5 great comments per day, 5 days a week, on the same 10-15 creators' posts is better than 20 random comments when you feel like it.

### HOW OFTEN:
- **Minimum viable:** 3-5 thoughtful comments per day
- **Growth mode:** 5-10 per day
- **Time investment:** 15-30 minutes total if you're focused

---

## What NOT to Do

- ❌ **"Great post!"** — Zero value, zero profile clicks. You're invisible.
- ❌ **"Totally agree 💯🔥"** — Same as above but with emojis.
- ❌ **Tagging random people.** — "Hey @person you need to see this!" feels desperate.
- ❌ **Pitching in comments.** — "I help people with exactly this! Book a call: [link]" — instant credibility destroyer.
- ❌ **Writing an essay.** — Keep comments to 3-8 lines. If it needs more, it's a post.
- ❌ **Commenting only on big accounts.** — Your comment gets buried under 400 others. Mix in smaller creators where you'll be seen.
- ❌ **Being contrarian for attention.** — Disagreeing without substance makes you look like a troll, not an expert.

## Output Format

When writing comments, present them as:

```
**Context:** [What the original post is about]
**Comment type:** [Which of the 5 types]
**Target reader:** [Who you want to click your profile after reading this]

---

[Comment text]

---

**Why this works:** [1-2 sentences on the strategy]
```

If the user shares a specific post they want to comment on, write 2-3 variations using different comment types so they can pick the best fit for the conversation.

## The Daily Comment Routine

For users who want a system:

1. **Morning (10 min):** Check the feeds of your 10-15 target creators. Find 3-5 new posts. Leave a value-add comment on each.
2. **Midday (5 min):** Check for replies to your comments. Respond to every one — this extends the conversation and increases your visibility.
3. **Evening (5 min):** One comment on a post from a potential client or peer in your space.

That's 20 minutes a day. Do this consistently for 30 days and track your profile views. They'll increase significantly — and with an optimized profile, those views turn into follows and DMs.

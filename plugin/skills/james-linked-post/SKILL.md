# LinkedIn Post Skill

## Overview

**Purpose:** Generate high-engagement LinkedIn posts from a topic, idea, or brief. Every post follows proven frameworks for maximum reach and interaction.

**Core Principles:**
- Optimal length: 800–1,000 characters (van der Blom research, 621,833+ posts analysed)
- Hook-first: LinkedIn truncates after ~140 characters — the hook must compel a "see more" click
- Influence-driven: Apply Robert Cialdini's principles of influence throughout
- Positive tone: Language is optimistic, bright, confident, and energising
- Australian English: All spelling and grammar follows Australian conventions
- GMAT-grade grammar: Clean, precise, error-free writing
- Factual accuracy: Every claim must be thoroughly fact-checked and correct

---

## Data-Backed Rules (Sources Below)

These rules come from Richard van der Blom's Algorithm Insights research (621,833+ posts, 34,000+ profiles, 50+ countries) and Socialinsider's LinkedIn Benchmarks 2025.

### Readability
- Write at a **4th-grade reading level**. Posts exceeding 10th-grade reading level get **35% less reach**
- Use **white space liberally** — short paragraphs of max 4 lines with line breaks between them boost readability and performance
- 91% of LinkedIn engagement happens on **mobile** — format for small screens

### Engagement Mechanics
- **Comments are 15x more valuable than likes** for algorithmic reach
- **Indirect comments** (replies to replies on your post) drive up to **2.4x increase in reach**
- The **first 60 minutes** after posting are critical — early quality interactions determine distribution
- A strong closing question or thought-provoking statement increases engagement by **20–40%**
- Posts now stay in feeds for **2–3 weeks** (up from ~24 hours in 2024) due to interest-graph distribution

### Content Format Reach Multipliers (Personal Profiles)
- Polls: **1.64x** (strongest format)
- Documents/Carousels: **1.45x** (and 2x average engagement)
- Images: **1.18x** (vertical images get 10% higher CTR than square)
- Video: **1.10x**
- Text-only: **0.88x** (lowest reach)

### What NOT to Do
- **No hashtags** — LinkedIn disabled hashtag following in Oct 2024. Hashtags have zero impact on reach. Using 6+ actively sabotages reach
- **No external links in the post body** — single links perform worst of all formats. If you must link, put it in the first comment
- **No excessive emojis or tags** — makes the post look spammy and harms algorithmic distribution
- **Do not post more than once per 12 hours** — consecutive posts cannibalise each other

### Posting Timing
- **Peak engagement:** Tuesday–Thursday, 8–10 AM (in the audience's timezone)
- **Secondary windows:** Monday/Friday, 12–2 PM
- **Weekends:** ~50% lower engagement

## Process

### 1. Determine Post Type

When given a topic, select the optimal post type:

| Type | Best For |
|------|----------|
| **Personal story/narrative** | Sharing lessons from real experiences |
| **Educational/how-to** | Teaching a process or framework |
| **Opinion/hot take** | Sparking conversation with a strong stance |
| **Listicle** | Sharing tips, tools, or techniques |
| **Celebration/milestone** | Announcing wins and achievements |
| **Case study** | Showcasing results with specifics |
| **Engagement bait** | Polls, questions, "agree or disagree?" |

If the type isn't obvious, choose the one most likely to drive engagement for the given topic.

### 2. Write the Hook (First 140 Characters)

The hook is the single most important element. It must stop the scroll and earn the "see more" click.

**Proven hook formats:**
```
- Timeline opener: "Last week, I..."
- Unexpected turn: Purpose sentence → surprise sentence
- Dream outcome: "How to X without Y"
- Best way: "The best way to {dream outcome}"
- Timeboxed achievement: "How I X in Y timeframe"
- Struggle list: "My biggest struggles as a {role} were:"
- Weird contrast: "It's weird {people} still use {tool} to {outcome}"
- Experience anchor: "I've been {action} for {timeframe}"
- Contrarian opener: State something you disagree with, then pivot
- Bold claim: Lead with a surprising statistic or result
```

**Hook checklist:**
- [ ] Under 140 characters?
- [ ] States benefit or creates curiosity?
- [ ] Includes proof or specificity (numbers, timeframes, results)?
- [ ] Uses PPP formula where possible (People + Pain + Payoff)?

### 3. Apply Cialdini's Influence Principles

Weave these throughout the post naturally:

1. **Reciprocity** — Give genuine value first (tips, frameworks, lessons learned) so readers feel compelled to engage back
2. **Social Proof** — Reference others doing the same, mention audience sizes, client numbers, or community adoption
3. **Authority** — Demonstrate expertise through specific experience, results, and domain knowledge
4. **Liking** — Be relatable, use humour, share vulnerability, and write conversationally
5. **Scarcity** — Create urgency or exclusivity ("few people know this", "this window is closing")
6. **Commitment & Consistency** — Ask small engagement actions (agree?, have you tried this?) that lead to larger ones

### 4. Structure the Body

**Formatting rules:**
- Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences max)
- Use line breaks liberally for readability
- Numbered lists (1, 2, 3 or emoji numbers) for steps and techniques
- Bold key phrases sparingly for emphasis
- Keep total length between 800–1,000 characters

**Tone:**
- Positive, optimistic, and confident
- Conversational authority — expert but approachable
- Direct and punchy — no waffle
- Active voice always

### 5. Craft the Sign-Off

The last line is the last thing they remember. Make it count.

**Sign-off options:**
- **Question** — Simple, engagement-driving ("What's worked for you?")
- **Callback to hook** — Reinforce the opening message
- **Challenge/invitation** — Ask readers to share their experience
- **Bold statement** — Confident, memorable closer
- **Yes/no question** — Lowest friction engagement

### 6. Final Quality Check

Before delivering, verify:

- [ ] Total length is 800–1,000 characters
- [ ] Hook is under 140 characters and compelling
- [ ] At least 2–3 Cialdini principles are present
- [ ] Language is positive, optimistic, and confident
- [ ] Written at ~4th-grade reading level (short words, short sentences)
- [ ] Formatted for mobile (short paragraphs, max 4 lines each, white space between)
- [ ] Australian English spelling throughout (e.g. organise, colour, programme)
- [ ] Grammar follows GMAT rules (parallelism, modifiers, subject-verb agreement)
- [ ] All claims are factually accurate and verifiable
- [ ] No LLM-isms (no em dashes, no "delve", no "in the world of", no "unlocking potential")
- [ ] No hashtags (they have zero impact on reach since Oct 2024)
- [ ] No external links in post body (put in first comment if needed)
- [ ] Ends with a question or thought-provoking statement (+20–40% engagement)
- [ ] Post type matches the topic effectively

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Em dashes (—) → Use colons, semicolons, or full stops
- "Let's delve into..." → Cut or replace with direct language
- "In the world of..." → Remove entirely
- "It's not just about X, it's about Y" → "X matters less than Y"
- "Unlocking the potential..." → "Making X work for Y"
- "At the end of the day..." → "Ultimately" or cut
- Generic claims without proof → Always add specifics
- Passive voice → Always use active voice
- Corporate jargon → Write like a human

## Workflow

**Step 1:** User provides a topic, idea, brief, or key points
**Step 2:** Determine the optimal post type
**Step 3:** Draft the post following all rules above
**Step 4:** Run the final quality check
**Step 5:** Present the post with a brief note on the type chosen, Cialdini principles used, and character count
**Step 6:** Iterate based on feedback
